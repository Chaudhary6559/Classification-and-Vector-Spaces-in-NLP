# Hello Vectors: Exploring Word Embeddings

This project explores **word vectors (word embeddings)** and how they represent semantic relationships between words in a numerical vector space.

Completed as **Assignment 3** of the *Classification and Vector Spaces in NLP* course, the notebook demonstrates word analogy prediction, similarity calculations, country-capital relationships, and dimensionality reduction using Principal Component Analysis (PCA).

---

## 📌 Project Overview

In Natural Language Processing (NLP), words can be represented as numerical vectors that capture aspects of their meaning and relationships with other words.

This assignment uses pretrained word embeddings to explore how mathematical operations and similarity measures can reveal semantic relationships between words.

The project also applies PCA to reduce high-dimensional word vectors to two dimensions for visualization.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

* Explore pretrained word embeddings.
* Predict relationships and analogies between words.
* Identify countries from their capital cities using word vectors.
* Calculate cosine similarity between word embeddings.
* Calculate Euclidean distance between vectors.
* Evaluate country-capital prediction accuracy.
* Apply PCA to reduce word-vector dimensionality.
* Visualize word embeddings in two dimensions.

## ⚙️ Methodology

### 1. Word Embeddings

Load pretrained word vectors and explore how words are represented in a numerical vector space.

### 2. Cosine Similarity

Calculate the cosine similarity between two word vectors to measure how closely their directions align.

### 3. Euclidean Distance

Calculate the Euclidean distance between word vectors to measure their geometric separation.

### 4. Country-Capital Prediction

Use vector relationships to identify the country associated with a given capital city.

### 5. Model Accuracy

Evaluate the country-capital prediction approach by comparing predictions against the expected answers.

### 6. PCA Visualization

Apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to reduce high-dimensional word embeddings to two dimensions and visualize their relationships.
